## Slot 2: Journal Club — Pilonidal Sinus Surgery OutcomesPaper (PMID 42633928)

**Full Citation:**

**Title:** Surgical Treatment Outcomes for Primary Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Single-Centre Retrospective Study of 2222 Patients (2006-2020).

**Authors:** Tokocin, Yigitbas, Yavuz, Gulcicek

**Journal:** International wound journal

**Publication Date:** 2026

**PMID:** 42633928

**Summary for our discussion:**
This single-centre retrospective study from a tertiary referral centre in Turkey analysed 2,222 patients with primary pilonidal sinus disease (PSD) who underwent surgery between January 2006 and December 2020, with a minimum follow-up of 5 years. Patients with recurrent disease were excluded, and nine surgical techniques were compared using Kaplan-Meier estimates and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression.

Key findings:
- 5-year cumulative recurrence: 7.9% (175/2222)
- Postoperative infection: 6.9% (154/2222)
- Overall recurrence: 10.2% (226/2222)
- Recurrence and infection differed significantly among techniques (both p < 0.001)
- Lowest recurrence rates: Karydakis flap (1.5%), Bascom cleft lift (2.3%), and Dufourmentel flap (2.4%)
- Median time to recurrence: 20 months (IQR 12-36); most recurrences occurred within the first 2 years
- Versus lay-open treatment, Karydakis flap (HR 0.19), Bascom cleft lift (HR 0.25), Limberg flap (HR 0.32), and Dufourmentel flap (HR 0.26) were independently associated with lower recurrence risk. Higher BMI was associated with increased recurrence risk (HR 1.05 per 1 kg/m²).
